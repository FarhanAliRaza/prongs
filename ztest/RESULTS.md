# Decisive experiment results (2026-08-02)

Environment: Linux x86-64 container, 4 CPU cores, CPython 3.11.15,
pytest 9.1.1, pytest-xdist 3.8.0 (execnet 2.1.2), Zig 0.16.0.
Suites generated by `benchmarks/generate.py`; harness `benchmarks/bench.py`,
best-of-N wall clock (N=3 for collection_heavy, else 2), `--tb=no -q`,
4 workers for every parallel runner.

## Correctness (the gate that had to pass first)

On the compatibility corpus (45 tests: fixtures at all scopes, parametrize,
skip/xfail/xpass, setup/teardown failures, capsys, monkeypatch, tmp_path,
threads, subprocesses, native imports, async via `asyncio.run`):

* collected node-ID parity with vanilla pytest: **100%**
* setup/call/teardown outcome parity: **100%** (at 1, 2, 8 workers; batch 2/5/16)
* failure fingerprint (type + source location) parity: **100%**
* exit-code parity: **100%**; zero lost tests, zero duplicates
* worker crash (`os._exit` mid-test): no hang, unrelated results intact,
  crashed test recorded as failed after one clean-worker retry

## Wall-clock (best of N, seconds)

| suite | pytest | xdist load-4 | xdist worksteal-4 | ztest prototype-4 | vs best xdist |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tiny (8k trivial tests) | 8.12 | 7.29 | 6.86 | **5.40** | **1.27× faster** |
| collection_heavy (3k params, heavy imports) | 3.45 | 3.51 | 3.34 | **2.72** | **1.23× faster** |
| uneven (400 tests, ms–2s) | 40.06 | 11.61 | 11.54 | **11.14** | 1.04× faster |
| cpu_heavy (64 × ~60ms pure Python) | 3.99 | 1.63 | 1.71 | **1.30** | **1.25× faster** |
| fixture_heavy (session/module/class fixtures) | 3.85 | 2.71 | **2.22** | 3.65 | **1.6× slower** |

Zig controller end-to-end (`ztest run -j 4 -- testbeds/tiny`): 6.33s —
within noise of xdist's best (6.86s) while doing all report handling over
the cross-language protocol; the pure-Python prototype shows the topology's
headroom (5.40s).

## Reading

* **The architectural bet holds.** Collect-once + prefork beats equally
  configured xdist wherever per-worker startup/collection or scheduling
  overhead matters (tiny, collection-heavy, cpu-heavy), because xdist pays
  imports + collection in every worker while ztest pays them once.
* **On xdist's home turf (uneven durations) we match it** — dynamic
  single-test refill with lookahead is at parity with worksteal.
* **fixture_heavy is the expected loss.** FIFO single-test refill scatters
  a module's tests across workers, repeating expensive module fixtures;
  worksteal's contiguous ranges preserve affinity by accident. This is
  precisely Milestone 6 (fixture-affinity work units). Not a blocker for
  continuing; it is the next scheduled piece of scheduler work.
* Go/no-go: the plan's bar — a clear advantage on collection-heavy and
  tiny suites — is met. Proceed toward the production runner.
